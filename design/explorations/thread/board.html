Ghazal is a poem.
And a thread.

01

Concept and name

The name already contains the system. We only had to make it move.

The word

غزل

A love poem built from couplets, and the act of spinning thread. A Kuwaiti wedding is both: a story told in verses, and one line pulled through a year of decisions. Arabic says it already: to compose verse is نَظْم, to string pearls on a line.

The rule

Gold is a verb.

Static structure is ink and rose. The gold line appears only where something is progressing, selected, connected or waiting. If a gold stroke carries no information, it is deleted. One thread per region of a screen, never a second.

The proof

One stroke, eight jobs.

2 px, round caps, drawn on from the inline-start edge over 640 ms. In Flutter it is a single ThreadPainter(path, progress, nodes) driven by an AnimationController: the same painter is the stepper, the tab underline, the loader and the timeline.

02

The one memorable idea

The Booking Thread. Certainty, drawn.

Five knots, one line, seen identically by the couple and the hotel.

Request, approval, deposit, balance, day. It is the booking stepper, the status, the payment schedule and the countdown on the client side, and the same booking on the hotel side. Each knot is tied only when something real has happened. It is how Ghazal makes the biggest purchase of the year feel certain.

05

Typography

Poppins and Cairo, and nothing else. The weight is the voice.

The decision

One pair. No display face.

The PRD names Poppins and Cairo. We kept exactly that. The thread is a monoline, and a Light 300 geometric sans is a monoline too: headlines are the same family as the interface, set at Light from 32 px, Regular below. Emphasis comes from size, weight (300 to 500) and numerals, not from a second voice. One font pair to ship against the 2 s load budget, one to keep in step across Flutter and web.

Figures are Cairo's Western digits in both languages: tabular, lining, one numeric voice from 12 px captions to 64 px hero numerals. Poppins has no tabular feature, so it never sets a number that must align.

Rules

Minimum: 20 px cap height (about 72 px wide); below that the knot drops, the line stays.
Weight: 300 above 28 px, 500 (Latin) or 600 (Arabic) below.
Motion: the line draws on once, 900 ms, from the inline-start edge. It never loops.
Never: recolour the line, put it above the letters, add a tagline, or outline the name.

Rules

Nothing blocks input. Nothing exceeds 500 ms except a deliberate thread or success moment (640 / 900). Nothing decorative loops.

Haptics: commit moments only: save, accept, pay, toggle, range tick.

13

Honest risks

What could make this direction fail, and what we would test first.

1Static screens undersell it.

The identity is motion. A screenshot shows a thin gold line; an AD reviewing PNGs may call it timid. Review on device, with the board's demos.

2Light type needs good photography.

Poppins and Cairo at 300 are elegant over calm images and weak over busy ones. Hero text therefore stays off photographs; only the wordmark and one caption sit on them.

3Arabic Light below 26 px.

Cairo 300 holds at display sizes but thins out small. Display 3 steps up to Regular; user-test the 34 px headline on low-end Android.

4Thin contrast margins.

Gold-700 on rose is 4.90 and gold-600 on rose is 3.10: pass, with no spare. Any tint change in the Rose ramp needs a re-check.

5Mixed-font numerals in Flutter.

Cairo digits inside Poppins lines need a formatter that emits per-span fonts and a 1.08 em size. Easy once; easy to forget in a new widget.

6Gold used sparingly may look flat.

The rule "gold is a verb" leaves most screens with one gold element. If the brand wants warmth, widen with photography, not with more gold.
